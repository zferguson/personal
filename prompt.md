You are a senior banking risk management advisor with deep expertise in OCC supervision, management information systems (MIS), enterprise risk management, risk appetite, KRI programs, board risk reporting, and de novo bank readiness.

Create a formal, leadership-ready document titled:

“Risk MIS Compliance Program Proposal & Implementation Plan”

The document is for Upstart and Upstart Bank, N.A. It will be used to propose a structured program for establishing and demonstrating compliance with OCC expectations for risk management information systems.

CONTEXT

Upstart Bank is preparing for OCC supervisory expectations associated with becoming an operating national bank. An enterprise KRI program is already being developed, including:

* KRI Framework
* KRI Standard
* KRI Procedure
* Enterprise KRI Register
* Risk taxonomy alignment
* Risk appetite linkage
* Board-, committee-, and operational-level KRI design

The MIS program must be broader than the KRI program.

Leadership also needs a structured understanding of all existing risk reporting and material risk metrics across Upstart and Upstart Bank, including reports provided to boards, management committees, risk committees, business functions, and control functions.

The target state should allow the organization to demonstrate that material risk information is accurate, timely, consistent, complete, relevant, appropriately controlled, appropriately escalated, and suitable for the decisions of the governing body receiving it.

REGULATORY RESEARCH REQUIREMENT

Before drafting the proposal, research the most current OCC requirements and supervisory guidance available as of today.

Prioritize primary OCC sources, including where relevant:

* Comptroller’s Handbook: Corporate and Risk Governance
* Comptroller’s Handbook: Bank Supervision Process
* Comptroller’s Handbook sections addressing management information systems
* Director’s Reference Guide
* Heightened Standards under 12 CFR Part 30, Appendix D, while clearly distinguishing provisions that legally apply only to covered institutions from broader supervisory principles
* OCC guidance on risk governance, risk appetite, board oversight, risk monitoring, data quality, risk aggregation, internal controls, and escalation
* Other current OCC or interagency guidance materially relevant to risk MIS

Do not describe a practice as an OCC “requirement” unless the source supports that characterization.

Clearly distinguish:

1. Regulatory or legal requirements
2. OCC supervisory expectations
3. Industry practices being recommended for Upstart

Cite primary sources throughout the document and provide the publication/version date where available.

OBJECTIVE

Develop a proposal for an enterprise Risk MIS Compliance Program that provides a defensible answer to these questions:

* What risk information is management and the Board relying upon?
* What risks does each report monitor?
* What decisions does each report support?
* What metrics and KRIs appear in each report?
* Where does the underlying data originate?
* Who owns the report, metric, and source data?
* How is accuracy verified?
* Is reporting timely?
* Are definitions and calculations consistent?
* Does reporting cover all material risks?
* Is the information appropriate for the audience receiving it?
* How are breaches, emerging risks, concentrations, issues, and adverse trends escalated?
* How does reporting connect to the enterprise risk taxonomy and risk appetite?
* How are reporting changes governed?
* How can Upstart demonstrate all of this to an OCC examiner?

Evaluate and refine the following proposed workstreams rather than accepting them uncritically:

1. OCC MIS Requirements and Controls Matrix

11. Examiner Evidence and Readiness Package

Identify any missing workstream that would be necessary for an OCC-defensible program.

WRITING REQUIREMENTS

Write for a senior banking risk-management audience.

Use precise regulatory terminology.

Do not use unnecessary consulting language.

Do not repeatedly describe the framework as “robust,” “holistic,” “best-in-class,” or similar language.

Do not claim that a specific template or process is required by the OCC unless OCC guidance actually requires it.

Where the OCC is principles-based, say so.

Challenge unnecessary documentation and duplicate controls.

The proposal should describe the minimum structure needed to create an OCC-defensible and operationally useful MIS program, not bureaucracy for its own sake.
