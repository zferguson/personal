Section 1: Integrating an Ever-Changing Data Landscape

Focus: How changing data sources, platforms, and business requirements affect integration and pipeline design.

Intended Response: Drawing on my experience with legacy enterprise systems and modern cloud platforms, I will discuss how data engineering has evolved while the fundamental integration challenges remain consistent. I will emphasize balancing modern platform capabilities with custom engineering, maintainability, and the preservation of consistent business definitions.

Section 2: Why the Hard Problems Never Go Away

Focus: Persistent challenges involving data quality, ownership, documentation, governance, and operational reliability.

Intended Response: I will discuss how technology alone cannot resolve organizational and business-definition problems. Using general examples from financial services risk analytics, I will explain why clearly defined metrics, accountable data owners, documented methodologies, and appropriate controls remain necessary regardless of the technology stack.

Section 3: Building Trustworthy Data Platforms

Focus: Defining pipeline success and establishing organizational trust in data.

Intended Response: I will emphasize that successful pipelines must produce reliable, business-ready information rather than simply move data between systems. Drawing on my experience developing executive risk reporting and KRI programs, I will discuss the importance of data lineage, consistent metric definitions, validation, and the ability to explain and defend analytical results.

Section 4: Data Engineering in the Age of AI

Focus: How generative AI, coding assistants, and agentic systems are changing data engineering and analytics.

Intended Response: I will discuss how AI is changing the way engineers develop pipelines and users interact with data, while increasing the importance of data quality and governance. I will also discuss the general principle of maintaining appropriate human oversight and execution controls when introducing AI into consequential business processes.

Closing: The Next Five Years of Data Engineering

Focus: What will change and what will remain consistent as data engineering evolves.

Intended Response: I expect AI and agentic systems to increasingly transform the interface and orchestration layers of data platforms, automating more development, querying, and analytical workflows. However, accountability for data quality, business definitions, governance, and the reliability of analytical outputs will remain fundamental.
